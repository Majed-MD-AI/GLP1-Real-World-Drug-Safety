# Evidence Brief

Status: v0.2 — initial pharmacovigilance analysis complete

## Question

Which pre-specified adverse events are disproportionately reported with semaglutide, liraglutide, and dulaglutide in FDA FAERS?

## Main findings

The initial analysis identified elevated reporting odds ratios across all 18 pre-specified drug–event combinations.

The strongest reporting signal was observed for liraglutide and pancreatitis:

- ROR 18.08
- 95% CI 17.44–18.75
- 3,237 matching reports

Pancreatitis was also disproportionately reported with:

- semaglutide: ROR 6.40 (95% CI 6.10–6.72)
- dulaglutide: ROR 5.85 (95% CI 5.59–6.13)

For semaglutide, elevated signals were also observed for cholelithiasis, vomiting, gastroparesis, cholecystitis, and nausea.

Gastroparesis produced relatively high ROR estimates for liraglutide and dulaglutide, but these estimates were based on very small numbers of matching reports and had substantially wider confidence intervals.

## Interpretation

These results represent pharmacovigilance reporting signals, not estimates of clinical risk.

A higher ROR means that an adverse event is reported disproportionately more often among reports involving a particular drug than among the comparator reports in FAERS.

It does not mean that the drug causes the event or that the patient's risk is increased by the same magnitude.

## What the findings cannot establish

FAERS disproportionality does not establish:

- causality
- incidence
- absolute risk
- comparative effectiveness
- treatment-effect magnitude
- a benefit–risk conclusion by itself

Important limitations include spontaneous-reporting bias, under-reporting, stimulated reporting, confounding by indication, co-medications, missing exposure denominators, and differences in time on market and drug utilization.

## Evidence needed next

A stronger confirmatory real-world study would ideally use longitudinal EHR, claims, or registry data with:

- a clearly defined source population
- incident-user design where possible
- an active comparator
- explicit time zero
- defined follow-up
- confounder adjustment
- sensitivity analyses
- potentially target-trial emulation

## Relevance to regulatory science and HTA

FAERS can support post-marketing signal detection and identify safety questions that warrant further investigation.

For regulatory, HTA, reimbursement, or clinical decision-making, spontaneous-report evidence should be integrated with longitudinal real-world evidence, clinical trials, and other higher-quality evidence sources rather than interpreted in isolation.

## Data snapshot

Primary analysis accessed the openFDA Drug Event API on 5 October 2026.

The openFDA metadata reported the dataset as last updated on 30 July 2026.

Because FAERS/openFDA is updated over time, report counts and disproportionality estimates may change when the analysis is re-run.
